De Haan Beach Promenade

De Haan (Le Coq), Belgian Coast, West Flanders  ·  51.2786° N, 3.0288° E  ·  11:23 AM–12:09 PM  ·  6 photos

NoteNo single named monument in this cluster's photos, but the location is identifiable with confidence from the built environment itself: the diamond-checkerboard paving, low-rise Anglo-Norman villas with red-tile gables, and orange-and-white striped beach cabins are distinctive to De Haan's Zeedijk/Normandiestraat promenade, which (unlike most of the Belgian coast) has no high-rise apartment blocks. The swimming/wading photos are the same beach, taken minutes later.

De Haan is the odd one out on the Belgian coast: a strip otherwise proudly dominated by 1970s apartment towers, this one small resort still looks much as it did a century ago, and appears determined to keep it that way out of pure spite. That's entirely by design. The Belgian state carved out a development concession here in 1889 under King Leopold II, and the founders promptly banned any building taller than the surrounding dunes and insisted on Anglo-Norman-style architecture -- rules the town has enforced ever since with the quiet ferocity of a very small committee. The result is the striped cabana huts, checkerboard promenade, and gabled villas visible in these photos: a Belle Époque seaside resort essentially preserved in amber while its neighbours modernised around it.

It's a fitting spot for a swim break on a barge-and-bike itinerary, since De Haan's whole identity is built around leisurely arrival: a steam tram line reached the dunes in 1886, before there was even a town to speak of, and the resort simply grew up around the passengers it delivered, which is either backwards or admirably efficient depending on one's outlook. The quiet, low-key beach scene in these frames -- families, striped tents, waders in a gray-green North Sea that has never once been mistaken for the Mediterranean -- is essentially the same postcard the town has been selling, unchanged, since the 1890s.

Eternity – Poseidon, Pigeons (Beaufort art trail)

De Haan, Belgian Coast, West Flanders  ·  51.2812° N, 3.0365° E  ·  12:52 PM  ·  2 photos

NoteThe bronze figure with birds perched along both outstretched arms is identifiable as 'Eternity – Poseidon, Pigeons' by Shanghai artist Xu Zhen, installed in De Haan for the Beaufort04 triennial in 2018 and kept in place afterward as a permanent public artwork along the dike.

This towering bronze -- a nude, bearded figure with arms flung wide, birds lined up along each arm like some sort of avian honour guard -- belongs to Beaufort, a triennial exhibition of contemporary art scattered along the entire Belgian coast, from De Panne to Knokke, since 2003, on the theory that a beach is improved by the occasional unexplained giant statue. Xu Zhen based the figure on the so-called Artemision Bronze, a 460 BC Greek statue (possibly Poseidon, possibly Zeus, and museums are still arguing about it with no sign of stopping) fished out of the Aegean seabed in 1928. The birds, on closer inspection, aren't gulls or pigeons at all but lacquered Peking ducks -- Xu Zhen's entirely deadpan mash-up of a Western classical icon with a Chinese culinary one, which is either high art or an elaborate joke, and possibly both.

It's a short walk east along the same sea dike from the beach cluster above, part of a scattered outdoor sculpture route that turns an ordinary promenade stroll into an inadvertent gallery visit, whether one signed up for that or not. Most Beaufort works are temporary and rotate out every three years; this one simply refused to leave, and has become one of the more photographed stops on De Haan's dike as a result.
