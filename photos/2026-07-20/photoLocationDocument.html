NoteThe tall neo-Gothic spire with corner pinnacles rising over the old town matches St. Nikolai's tower — at roughly 90 m the tallest landmark on the Flensburg skyline — and the cluster's coordinates sit directly on the harbor promenade with a clear sightline across the water to the church, consistent with the photo's framing.

Flensburg's harbour is really a long fjord arm putting on airs, and this evening shot looks across it from the western quay toward the old town, with St. Nikolai's spire piercing the skyline above the rooftops like it has somewhere important to be. The church's tower has burned down and been rebuilt more than once -- the current 90-metre neo-Gothic spire dates only to 1878, designed by the Berlin architect Johannes Otzen after lightning destroyed its predecessor, lightning apparently having strong opinions about church architecture -- though the church itself traces back to the 16th century. A carillon installed in 1909 still rings out from 60 metres up several times a day, entirely unbothered by any of this history.

The harbour below has been the reason for Flensburg's existence since the Middle Ages, when it grew rich shipping grain, then later rum, sugar and salt from the Caribbean colonies, a supply chain with considerably more moral complexity than the postcards let on. Sailboats and the low silhouettes of moored classic ships still crowd the water in front of the old merchant houses, and the working harbour atmosphere -- visible here in the dinghy cutting a wake across the calm evening water -- is largely unchanged from the postcards of a century ago, which have apparently seen no reason to update themselves.

Flensburg harbour promenade near the Phänomenta

NoteThe blue-tiled building reflected in the water carries a partial 'PHÄNOMENTA' sign, identifying it as the Phänomenta science center's harborside building; the timing (nine minutes after the previous photo) and short distance from cluster 0 place this along the same stretch of harbor promenade.

A few minutes' walk up the quay from the St. Nikolai view brings the Phänomenta into frame -- Flensburg's hands-on science museum, housed in a converted harbourside building whose blue-tiled facade remains unmistakable even reduced to a rippling reflection at low sun, which is more than can be said for most buildings. The museum opened in 1996 as one of Germany's first 'Mitmach' (hands-on) science centres, built on the entirely sensible premise that visitors learn physics and perception better by touching exhibits than by reading a stern placard next to them.

This photo, taken low over the waterline with the wooden piling and dark harbour water dominating the foreground, catches the everyday rhythm of the promenade rather than the museum itself -- reflections of the old warehouses and the tide line worn into the stonework are as much the subject as any single building, a fitting way to end an evening stroll along water that has defined this city for eight centuries and shows no sign of losing interest.
